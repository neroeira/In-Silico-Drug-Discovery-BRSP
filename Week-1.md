# Research Paper Summary

## Unveiling the Anti-Obesity Potential of Kemuning (Murraya paniculata): A Network Pharmacology Approach

Obesity has been spreading like a global pandemic since 1975, significantly leading to diet-related chronic diseases such as diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, heart and blood vessel disorders, organ fat, and several other disorders, including cancer. Approaches using herbal plants include kemuning (_Murraya paniculata_), which has been utilized due to its anti-obesity, analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-diarrheal, anti-bacterial, and anti-hyperglycemic properties from flavonoids, coumarins, alkaloids, saponins, and tannins. Even so, no comprehensive studies have explored the mechanism of _M. paniculata_ as an anti-obesity agent using a network pharmacological and in vitro assay approach.


This study aimed to analyze the mechanism of active compounds found in _M. paniculata_ using a metabolomic and network pharmacology approach using the skyline queries technique, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and in vitro tests.

The protein targets from the OMIM and STITCH databases, respectively associated with obesity and _M. paniculata_, were screened based on active compounds and the "Homo sapiens" category. The data were then analyzed through PPI network construction (STRING + Cytoscape), skyline query technique, GO enrichment analysis, metabolite profiling, ADME prediction, and molecular docking (AutoDock Vina + PyMOL). Ultimately, the core findings were confirmed through in vitro testing of preadipocyte cells cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium.

Network pharmacology identified 1,332 overlapping obesity-_M. paniculata_ target proteins, with EP300, PPARG, and PPARGC1A emerging as the top dominant hub genes driving fat cell differentiation, AMPK, and glucagon signaling. Out of 16 LC-MS-identified active compounds, 10 met Lipinski’s rule. Molecular docking revealed that (1R,9S)-5-[(E)-2-(4-Chlorophenyl)vinyl]-11-(5-pyrimidinylcarbonyl)-7,11-diazatricyclo[7.3.1.02,7]trideca-2,4-dien-6-one had the highest binding affinities for PPARG (−8.8 kcal/mol) and EP300 (−8.7 kcal/mol), showing structural stability in 100-ns molecular dynamics. In vitro, the extract was safe up to 125 μg/ml and significantly decreased adipocyte differentiation by downregulating PPARG and EP300 mRNA expression in 3T3-L1 cells.

This study introduces a novel computational workflow combining network pharmacology with a skyline query technique and the block-nested loop (BNL) to identify dominant targets and successfully bridges molecular docking with in vitro validation. However, the biological evaluation is restricted to a 3T3-L1 white adipocyte model. The extract's therapeutic effects on brown adipose tissue (thermogenesis) and complex in vivo systems remain unverified. 

This study demonstrated how complex botanical extracts like _M. paniculata_ leverage multi-compound synergy to systematically modulate metabolic networks rather than relying on a single drug. By pairing a novel skyline-query network computational workflow with in vitro validation, it establishes a cost-effective blueprint for modern drug discovery. This approach rapidly decodes traditional herbal mechanisms, mapping active metabolites to critical obesity hub genes (PPARG and EP300) before advancing to resource-intensive in vivo clinical trials.
